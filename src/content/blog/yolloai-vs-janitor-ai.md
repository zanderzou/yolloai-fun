<p class="article-lede">Yollo AI and Janitor AI both let people enter fictional character stories. Their current official descriptions make a more useful distinction than “which bot is freer?” Yollo presents character chat, AI still images and short video in one product. Janitor's developer describes an interactive-story community where readers and writers find characters, shape scenes and create worlds. Its September 2026 Android release notes also mention scripts and lorebooks. This comparison stays with those verifiable claims; the exact model, API and subscription options of a given account should be checked in-app.</p>

## At a glance

| Task | Yollo AI | Janitor AI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First action | Browse a character or create a bot | Select a story/character or build a fictional world |
| Creative emphasis | Roleplay connected to image and short video | Interactive fiction and conversation-led scene control |
| Structured world detail | Test persona and advertised memory in the current account | Official app update lists scripts and lorebooks |

## Story control versus visual continuity

Janitor's official mobile listing speaks to readers and writers who want to choose a genre, find a character and influence what happens next. The developer's release notes refer to scripts and lorebooks, which may give creators more control over a setting and its rules. But the listing does not specify what every account can edit, the exact limits, or whether a web feature matches the Android beta. Check the live interface rather than borrowing details from third-party tutorials.

Yollo's site emphasizes a different arc: find or design an adult fictional character, chat, then produce an image or short clip. It also advertises personas and long-term memory. The risk is discontinuity: a character can be coherent in text yet change face or scene when turned into media. Measure both narrative control and visual consistency.

## A fair, reproducible scene

Use a fictional adult museum curator called Eden preparing a midnight exhibition. Give both services the same initial brief: a missing blue sketchbook, a locked gallery and a deadline at dawn. Ask for twelve turns of dialogue. After a detour, ask each character what is missing and what they plan to do next. Rate whether the response remembers facts, respects the established point of view, advances the plot and leaves your character's actions under your control.

On Janitor, inspect whether the current version offers scripts or lorebooks for your account and whether a short world rule improves the next scene. On Yollo, request a still of the gallery and a short video where available, then judge whether the same adult fictional character, sketchbook and lighting persist. Record delays, failed attempts and the exact paid actions. These steps are an evaluation framework, **not** an invented test result.

## What not to assume about models and APIs

Older tutorials often frame Janitor AI around external model-provider keys. That may describe a particular configuration or an earlier product version, but the current official app listing does not establish which options are available to every user. If an account exposes a provider choice, identify who processes the chat, whether an external API bill is separate, and whether changing providers changes continuity. Do not present an unverified API setup as a universal requirement.

Yollo also advertises multiple model options. Test the exact model visible in your own account; a brand name or “long-term memory” label does not prove a specified recall rate. The same harmless scene across two runs is more informative than a feature list.

## Privacy, eligibility and cost

Both services can accumulate intimate-seeming fictional conversations; keep real names, addresses, credentials and other secrets out. Janitor's Google Play data-safety panel says the app may collect personal information and app activity, and offers a deletion request, but this developer-provided summary is not a substitute for reading the current privacy policy. Yollo's Terms describe user-created content, public sharing options, paid subscriptions and usage fees. They also prohibit access by people located in or resident in Mainland China or Hong Kong. Do not circumvent those restrictions.

Compare a real week of use: messages, chosen model, any scripts/lorebook access, still images, video retries and in-app purchases. A free starting screen does not prove the full workflow is free on either service.

## Verdict

Choose Yollo AI if visualizing a roleplay scene is essential and its chat-to-image/video path proves consistent and affordable. Choose Janitor AI if your priority is building or inhabiting a longer interactive story, and its present script/lorebook controls give you the necessary narrative flexibility. The stronger choice is the one that passes your own same-scene test while keeping data and spending within clear limits.
